## Why partnership is the leverage point

`/partnership/` frames partnership as a bond in which people unite "not only to
share profits and losses" but for a collective journey. The scale reported:
**5,002+** deeds created worldwide, **12,698+** business partners globally,
**396+** localities with active partnerships.

The doctrinal grounding is attributed to **Syedna al-Qadi al-Noman RA**:
partnerships conforming to Shariat, formed by mutual consent with proper legal
documentation, strengthen unity and distance participants from negative
influence. *(The page dates this 1438 H, which cannot be the author's own date —
al-Qadi al-Nu'man d. 363 AH. It is most likely the date of the guidance in which
the teaching was relayed. The site does not clarify. Flagged, not resolved.)*

**Strategic read:** partnership is the cheapest form of leverage available to
someone with more capability than capital. The doctrine's entire apparatus here
exists because partnership is also the highest-variance instrument — it either
multiplies or it detonates, and documentation is what selects the branch.

## The two compliant structures

### Mushaarakat — "collective endeavors"
- Each partner contributes **capital**
- Ownership proportionate to investment
- Profits **and responsibilities** distributed equitably

*Use when:* both sides bring money and both intend to work.

### Mudaarabat — "skillful synergy"
- One party provides **capital**, the other provides **expertise**
- Profits shared by agreement
- The capital provider's loss is **limited to capital invested**

*Use when:* you have capability and no capital, or capital and no operator.
This is the structure a founder without money should understand cold — it is
equity-like leverage without interest and without a fixed obligation.

### What is excluded by construction
- Interest / **viyaaj**
- Fixed guaranteed profit to either side — profit must be a genuine share of
  actual outcome, or the structure is void of its logic
- Loss distribution that does not track the agreed basis

## The three pillars of trust
From `/business/business-partnerships-that-work-the-3-pillars-of-trust/`:

1. **Transparency** — open goals and open finances; concerns raised without blame
2. **Reliability** — deadlines met, delivery made, responsiveness maintained
3. **Mutual respect** — strengths recognised, no micromanagement, wins celebrated

Five-step protocol from the same source:
align on **values before contracting** → document the agreement → hold regular
check-ins *outside* of crises → address problems transparently → invest
relationally (meet in person, actively seek feedback).

Named tactics: shared dashboards; under-promise and over-deliver.

## Competitor → collaborator
From `/business/from-competitors-to-collaborators-...`:
find partners with overlapping customer bases → seek complementary rather than
duplicate strengths → verify shared values and vision → set explicit goals and
success metrics **before** starting → open communication channels → stay
flexible. Precedent cited: Apple + Nike (Nike+ iPod Sport Kit).

## Cross-industry alliances
From `/business/the-importance-of-cross-industry-partnerships-...` and
`/business/strategic-alliances/`: break sector silos, pool R&D cost, set
transparent agreements on data-sharing and revenue distribution, define
governance explicitly, partner locally for market credibility, distribute
expansion risk. Each party retains independence — that is the definitional
difference from a merger.

## The deed process (six steps)
`/partnership/`. Note that this is a commercial instrument routed through
religious authority — the design is deliberate.

1. Online deed presentation through **Amil Saheb**
2. **Idarah** preliminary approval
3. Signature addition and document upload
4. Central office processing
5. **Araz** presentation at **Ḥaḍrat ʿĀliyah**
6. **Misaal Shareef** finalisation

## Pre-signature checklist
- [ ] Values alignment tested before terms discussed
- [ ] Structure named explicitly: Mushaarakat or Mudaarabat
- [ ] Profit share is a share of actual outcome, not a fixed guarantee
- [ ] Loss basis stated and proportionate
- [ ] Exit and dissolution mechanics written
- [ ] Review cadence scheduled outside of crisis
- [ ] Deed routed through the six-step process where applicable
