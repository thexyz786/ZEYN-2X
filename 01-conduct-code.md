## The seven hard rules

### R1 — Do not spend more than you earn
Stated flatly on `/home-old/`. Paired with a household-budgeting curriculum and
a directive from **Syedna Mohammed Burhanuddin RA** to properly balance
household expenditure.

**Why it compounds:** personal burn is the hidden liability on every founder's
balance sheet. A founder who needs the business to fund lifestyle cannot make
long-horizon decisions, which is precisely where the returns are.

### R2 — Save a minimum of one third of income
Stated as a figure on `/home-old/`; taught in the Household Budgeting course.

**Why it compounds:** a 33% reserve rate is what converts a downturn from an
existential event into an inconvenience. It is also the only source of
non-dilutive, non-interest-bearing growth capital available to a small operator.

### R3 — Run on cash, not credit
There is an entire named course for this: *Transform Your Business from Credit
to Cash* (`/home-old/`). The partnership survey reports **691** businesses that
moved from credit to cash operations.

**Why it compounds:** credit-run businesses grow revenue and shrink optionality
at the same time. Cash operations are slower at the start and unkillable later.
Related: interest (*viyaaj*) is what the system routes around — **453** reported
avoiding it.

> **Rev 2 correction.** Rev 1 treated **Qardan Hasana** as a doctrinal note. It
> is not — it is one of the four institutional arms of DBDEA, and it reports
> **50,000+ interest-free financial cases facilitated**. R3 is therefore not a
> counsel of austerity. It says: refuse interest-bearing credit *because a
> genuine interest-free alternative exists at scale.* Register 05 covers access.

### R4 — Every partnership in writing. Including family
`/home-old/` states it; a named course teaches the clauses; the TAP testimonial
from Abidhussain bhai is specifically about learning this. **454** reported
avoiding court litigation as a result.

**Why it compounds:** the undocumented family partnership is the single most
common way a profitable Bohra business destroys itself in generation two. A deed
costs a week. The dispute costs a decade.

### R5 — Work 6–12 months inside the industry before launching into it
`/start-business` gives this as a pre-launch requirement, not a suggestion.

**Why it compounds:** it converts the most expensive category of learning
(capital-at-risk mistakes) into the cheapest (salaried observation). Compresses
the failure curve by a year at near-zero cost.

### R6 — Seek counsel; rank elders' experience above formal education
`/`, `/idd/`, and the advice article. **Syedna Mufaddal Saifuddin TUS** ranks
elders' transmitted experience above education explicitly.

**Why it compounds:** advice is the only input with no marginal cost and no
dilution. The doctrine's mutual-aid basis means the network is obliged to give
it — an asset most founders never draw on.

### R7 — Do not compromise ethics for a transaction
`/grow-a-business/`: compromise brings detrimental long-term repercussions;
upholding builds trust and credibility, which is what secures long-run success.

**Why it compounds:** see `00 §5`. Ethics here is infrastructure, not sentiment.
The verified-community model (`/dbohra/`) means a reputation loss is a
distribution loss.

## Employer-side duties
From the Ashara Mubaraka article. These bind you the moment you have one employee.

- Suspend operations fully for the ten days — an edict, not a discretionary holiday
- Prepare **non-mumin** staff: explain the observance, give exact dates, communicate
  kindly and clearly. The *manner* is part of the duty.
- Prepare **mumin** staff: enable attendance, verify compliance with edicts, assist
  with attire, facilitate **Qardan Hasana**
- Train staff that no emergency justifies breaching the closure
- Use the closure window for staff upskilling rather than treating it as dead time

**Strategic read:** a fully-planned ten-day annual shutdown forces documented
process and delegated authority. Most small businesses cannot survive ten days
without the owner. Building one that can is a growth prerequisite disguised as a
religious obligation.
