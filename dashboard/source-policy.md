# Source Policy: Daily Brief on Politics and World Affairs

Prepared 5 October 2026 for a Los Angeles reader with Gulf and South Asian ties, reading at the level of The Economist and Foreign Affairs. Zero American infotainment.

## How judgments were made

Ratings come from Ad Fontes Media (reliability, 0 to 64), Media Bias/Fact Check (MBFC factual grades), NewsGuard (0 to 100) and the Reuters Institute Digital News Report (DNR) 2025 and 2026, plus IPSO, Ofcom and BBC ECU records, the RSF 2026 index, the State Media Monitor typology and documented scandals. Reputation alone counted for nothing. Where evaluators disagree, both are shown.

Context figures that frame everything: the 2026 Digital News Report puts overall trust in news at 37 percent globally, 25 percent in the United States (seventh lowest of 48 markets), 30 percent in the United Kingdom and 39 percent in India. RSF 2026 ranks Qatar 75th, Israel 116th, Pakistan 153rd, India 157th, the UAE 158th, Egypt 161st and Saudi Arabia 176th of 180. Pew's June 2025 study of 30 US outlets found Republicans trusted only eight and Democrats 23; The Wall Street Journal and Forbes were the only two on both lists.

## Tier 1: Wires and primary documents

Wires lead every item. Primary documents (court filings, UN and government releases, central bank statements, company filings, treaty texts) outrank any report about them.

## Tier 3: Regional specialists

Standing constraints: India (RSF 157) faces intensifying judicial harassment of independent media and the Press Council of India has had no sitting council since 5 October 2024. Pakistan (RSF 153) logged 129 verified violations against journalists from April 2025 to March 2026, most under the amended PECA law, with the military as the unspoken censor. Every Gulf English daily is state-owned or ruling-family-adjacent under cybercrime laws that criminalize criticism.

## Tier 5: Analysis and long-form for the daily deeper-reading slot

Funding sets the caveat. Chatham House, Carnegie (Candid Platinum seal), Crisis Group (about half from governments), Stimson (full donor list, 18 percent foreign governments) and Baker Institute (46 percent Rice endowment, lists gifts above 50 dollars) all disclose. ECFR was founded with Open Society money. CSIS historically earned Transparify's one-star grade; Brookings three stars. Flags:

- Brookings: over 30 million dollars from Qatar in 15 years; president John Allen resigned in 2022 amid an FBI probe of lobbying for Doha. Corroborate any Gulf piece.
- IISS: Bahrain's ruling family supplied 30.2 million pounds, 33.6 percent of revenue, 2010-15, under a secrecy agreement (Bahrain Watch). Discount its Bahrain and Gulf security output.
- ORF (India): founded by the Ambanis; Reliance still roughly 65 percent of inflows. Read as Delhi-establishment and Reliance-adjacent.
- Arab Center Washington DC: all but 900 dollars of 2.26 million in donations came from its Doha parent. Read as Qatar's academic voice, paired with a Saudi or Emirati counterpart.
- Atlantic Council (added): the UAE is its largest foreign donor; use with the same pairing rule.

## Blocklist

Partisan cable and talk: Fox News (NewsGuard 57, red; 787.5 million dollar Dominion settlement; DNR US trust 31 percent); MS NOW, formerly MSNBC (NewsGuard red; opinion-led); CNN opinion and panel programming (the CNN website scores Ad Fontes 41.90 and is allowed for corroboration, the shows are not); Newsmax and OAN (Pew: trusted mainly by people who distrust national news; repeated defamation settlements).

Tabloids: New York Post (MBFC Mixed), Daily Mail and MailOnline (633 IPSO complaints in 2025 with accuracy breaches on tax and Net Zero stories; 51 percent UK distrust), The Sun (least trusted UK brand, 67 percent distrust), Daily Express.

Outrage and clickbait digital: Breitbart, Daily Wire (both trusted mostly by distrusters of national news, Pew); HuffPost (MBFC Mostly Factual, blocked for partisan selection and lifestyle filler, not inaccuracy); Daily Kos (MBFC Mixed); Raw Story (MBFC High on facts, blocked as aggregation built for outrage); Newsweek and Epoch Times (added: volume aggregation and Falun Gong advocacy respectively); Zero Hedge (added). Removed from the draft: BuzzFeed News, which closed in 2023 and no longer publishes.

State propaganda: RT (NewsGuard 20, Ofcom licence revoked 2022), Sputnik (17.5), Press TV (Ofcom revoked 2012 after a coerced-interview fine), CGTN (NewsGuard 44.5 and 0/10 on connected TV; Ofcom revoked 2021), Global Times, Xinhua and TASS as sole sources (allowed only for verbatim official statements, labelled), Al Mayadeen (added, Hezbollah-aligned), Middle East Monitor (added, advocacy not reporting).

Indian hyper-nationalist channels: Republic (named in the Mumbai TRP charge sheet; case later withdrawn; Alt News repeatedly fact-checked), Zee News (32 Alt News misinformation instances in 2022; reported in May 2025 that India had "captured Islamabad"), Times Now (Alt News: Times Group led 2022 misinformation count; ran four-year-old footage as live combat in May 2025). Added: ANI as sole source (EU DisinfoLab documented hundreds of stories quoting non-existent think tanks and defunct fake outlets), OpIndia, Swarajya, WION.

Also blocked: aggregators and content farms (may route, never be cited), press-release wires (PR Newswire, Business Wire, GlobeNewswire) as sole source, and social media posts, which are leads until a Tier 1 or Tier 2 outlet verifies them.

Not blocked despite expectation: The Telegraph (most IPSO code breaches in 2025) is simply omitted; it is not infotainment, just unnecessary when the FT and Times exist.

## Corroboration rules

1. State-funded outlet on its funder or the funder's rivals: Al Jazeera on Qatar, Hamas, Saudi Arabia or the UAE; BBC on UK policy; France 24 on the Sahel; DW on Germany; NHK on Japan; any Gulf daily on its own government or on Qatar; Anadolu on anything. Require one Tier 1 wire or a Tier 2 outlet funded by a different state.
2. Outlet on its owner: Bloomberg on Michael Bloomberg; WSJ, Times and NY Post on News Corp; WaPo on Amazon; NDTV on Adani; The Print or ORF on Reliance; The National on Mansour bin Zayed's holdings; Politico on Axel Springer; SCMP on Alibaba. Second source mandatory; prefer a wire.
3. Casualty figures: publish only with attribution and a second channel. For Gaza, the Lancet found no evidence of inflation in Ministry of Health counts in 2023 and a 2025 household survey found undercounting (75,200 estimated violent deaths against 49,090 recorded), so quote MoH figures with UN or WHO confirmation and never accept a higher or lower figure from a single party, including the IDF.
4. Sanctions, designations and indictments: cite the primary document (OFAC, EU Official Journal, UN Security Council, court docket) or treat as unconfirmed.
5. Anonymous-sourced scoops: label as such; need a second outlet with independent sourcing or an on-record confirmation before the brief treats it as fact. Exception: AP, Reuters and AFP scoops run with a "single-source" tag.
6. India-Pakistan military claims and Gulf-Iran incidents: require one source from each side plus a wire; after the May 2025 television fabrications, no Indian or Pakistani broadcaster counts as a source.
7. Numbers that sound like PR (circulation, viewership, GDP forecasts from interested parties): primary document or omit.
8. Retractions travel: any outlet with a retraction in the prior 12 months (The Wire, 2022 precedent; BBC after the Ofcom ruling) gets a second source on the same subject for a year.
